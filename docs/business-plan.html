Business plan · October 2026

Kopor

The business book, payments and advisor for small businesses, groups and institutions in Sierra Leone, built to expand across West Africa.

1Executive summary

Most businesses in Sierra Leone keep their records in a paper notebook, or in their head. Debts given on credit are forgotten or disputed, owners rarely know their real profit, and without records they cannot borrow: only 17% of SMEs have a formal loan or credit line.

Kopor is a mobile app that replaces the notebook. It records credit, sales, expenses and stock in seconds, works fully offline, understands Krio, sends WhatsApp and SMS payment reminders, runs osusu and dues groups, issues invoices that customers can pay by mobile money, and turns daily records into a loan-ready report with a business health score.

2Problem

  • Credit is everyday business, and it leaks. Traders sell on "trust" to regular customers. A notebook cannot remind anyone, total balances, or settle a dispute about what was paid.
  • Owners cannot see profit. Cash, Orange Money, Afrimoney and household money mix together. Stock runs out without warning.
  • Groups run on trust and memory. Osusu, association dues and school contributions are tracked by one person, often on paper.
  • No records, no credit. Banks and microfinance institutions see small businesses as high-risk because there is no reliable data on their sales and repayments.

3Solution & product

Kopor is an offline-first app for Android phones and browsers. Data is saved on the phone first and synced to the cloud when internet is available, so a shop never stops working when the network drops.

4Market

Micro, small and medium enterprises make up about 98.5% of businesses in Sierra Leone, provide about 84% of employment and around 70% of GDP. Most are informal: in a national survey, 66% of informal firms were not registered with any formal institution, and 37 to 45% of businesses are petty traders.

There were about 8.94 million mobile connections at the end of 2025, roughly one per person, and 1.85 million internet users (20.8%). Smartphone use is concentrated in towns and among younger owners, which defines our first target segment.

Market size (estimate)

No official count of all businesses exists, so we estimate from the bottom up: of the several hundred thousand micro-businesses, we target those in towns whose owner or a family member uses a smartphone. We estimate about 100,000 reachable businesses in the first three years, plus groups and institutions. Liberia and Guinea add a similar market from year 3. These are planning estimates, to be refined with pilot data and the 2022 Census of Business Establishments.

7Go-to-market

Phase 1 · Freetown pilot (months 1–3)

50 shops in central markets and 5 osusu groups. Weekly visits, training in Krio, feedback collected in person. Goal: prove that shops keep recording and that reminders collect real money.

Phase 2 · Scale in Sierra Leone (months 4–18)

Market agents in Freetown, then Bo, Kenema and Makeni, paid a commission for every shop still active after 30 days. Partnerships with market and trader associations, osusu leaders and one or more microfinance institutions. Local radio and WhatsApp campaigns in Krio.

Phase 3 · West Africa (year 3)

Liberia (English) and Guinea (French) using the same product. Monime's coverage and local partners decide the order.

Built-in growth

Every reminder, statement, invoice and group update a user sends carries the Kopor name to customers and members, many of whom run businesses themselves.

8Competition

  • Paper notebooks and memory: free and familiar, the real competitor. Kopor must be faster than writing.
  • Khatabook and OkCredit (India): proved the digital credit book at scale (Khatabook raised about $187M). Not available for Sierra Leone's currency, languages or payment systems. OkCredit reportedly earned no operating revenue for two years: we charge from the start.
  • Kippa (Nigeria): raised about $11.6M and reached about 500,000 merchants, then reportedly cut back in 2023. Lesson: stay lean and focus on what users pay for.
  • Mobile money operators: Orange Money offers credit products (Lajor). Kopor is network-neutral and works with every wallet, which telcos cannot easily match.

9Operations & technology

  • Offline-first app (React, TypeScript), installable from the web and on Google Play (Capacitor).
  • Cloud on Supabase (Postgres) with row-level security for every staff role; secrets are kept on the server only.
  • Payments through Monime; AI through free or low-cost models (Groq, Google Gemini) with daily limits per business.
  • Quality: 41 unit tests, 13 database security tests and 37 end-to-end browser tests run on every change.
  • Running costs are low: under $100 per month for the first thousands of users.

10Regulation & trust

Under the National Payment Systems Act 2022, providing payment services in Sierra Leone requires a Bank of Sierra Leone licence. Kopor is designed so it does not need one at launch: it never holds or moves money itself. Businesses connect their own account with Monime, which is licensed. If Kopor later offers wallets or lending, it will apply for the required licence or partner with a licensed institution.

Privacy: records stay on the phone unless the owner turns on the cloud; access is limited by role; backups can be encrypted; and users can delete their account. The privacy policy and terms will be reviewed by a Sierra Leone lawyer before launch.

Unit economics (estimates)

Cost to acquire an active business in year 1 is about $3.50 (agents, commissions and marketing ÷ 5,000). A Pro subscriber pays about $39 a year; with 6–10% of businesses converting plus group and referral revenue, each active business is worth about $8–10 over its lifetime. The target is a lifetime value at least three times the acquisition cost, to be confirmed in the pilot.
